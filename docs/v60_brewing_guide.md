# Hario V60 Pour Over - Complete Brewing Guide

## Quick Reference: Baseline Recipe

- **Coffee**: 20g
- **Water**: 300ml (1:15 ratio)
- **Grind**: Medium-fine (like table salt)
- **Temperature**: 92°C
- **Total Time**: 2:30-3:30 minutes

---

## Understanding the Variables

### 1. Grind Size
**Impact**: Controls extraction speed and strength

- **Finer grind** → More extraction → Stronger, fuller body, more bitterness
- **Coarser grind** → Less extraction → Lighter, brighter, more acidity

**Adjust when:**
- Coffee tastes **sour/weak** → Grind finer
- Coffee tastes **bitter/harsh** → Grind coarser
- **Target consistency**: Table salt to slightly finer

---

### 2. Water Temperature
**Impact**: Controls extraction speed and flavor balance

| Temperature | Effect | Best For |
|------------|--------|----------|
| 85-88°C | Lower extraction, sweeter | Dark roasts |
| 90-92°C | Balanced extraction | Medium roasts |
| 92-94°C | Higher extraction, sweet spot | Light roasts |
| 95-96°C | Maximum extraction | Very light roasts |

**Adjust when:**
- Coffee tastes **sour/underdeveloped** → Increase temperature
- Coffee tastes **bitter/over-extracted** → Decrease temperature
- Want **more sweetness** → Try 92-94°C

---

### 3. Brew Ratio (Coffee:Water)
**Impact**: Controls strength and body

| Ratio | Effect | Character |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1:14 | Stronger | Full body, intense |
| 1:15 | Standard | Balanced (recommended start) |
| 1:16 | Lighter | More nuanced, delicate |
| 1:17 | Weak | Risk of thin/watery |

**Adjust when:**
- Coffee tastes **weak/watery** → Use 1:14 or 1:15
- Coffee tastes **too strong/muddy** → Use 1:16 or 1:17

---

### 4. Total Brew Time
**Impact**: Overall extraction level

- **Under 2:30 min** → Under-extracted (sour, thin, sharp)
- **2:30-3:30 min** → Ideal range (balanced, sweet)
- **Over 3:30 min** → Over-extracted (bitter, dry, astringent)

**Control brew time by:**
- Grind size (primary control)
- Pour rate (secondary control)
- Water temperature (minor effect)

---

### 5. Pour Technique
**Impact**: Extraction evenness and clarity

**Bloom (First Pour):**
- Use 2-3x coffee weight in water (40-60g for 20g coffee)
- Wait 30-45 seconds
- Optional: Gentle stir to ensure all grounds are wet
- Purpose: Release CO2, prepare for even extraction

**Main Pours:**
- **Continuous pour**: Steady stream, easier control, clean cup
- **Pulse pours**: Multiple separate pours, more complexity
- **Pour pattern**: Gentle circles from center outward
- **Avoid**: Pouring on the filter edges (causes channeling)

**Agitation:**
- **Gentle swirl** after final pour → Flattens bed, even drainage
- **More turbulence** → More extraction
- **Less turbulence** → Cleaner, lighter cup

---

## Additional Critical Factors

### Water Quality
- Use **filtered water**
- Ideal TDS: 75-150 ppm
- Too soft → Flat taste
- Too hard → Chalky, mineral taste
- Try bottled water (Volvic, Third Wave Water) if tap water is problematic

### Coffee Freshness
- **Peak sweetness**: 7-21 days after roasting
- **Very fresh** (under 7 days): Can be difficult to extract evenly
- **Old** (over 30 days): Loses sweetness and complexity

### Pre-warming
- Rinse filter with hot water before brewing
- Removes paper taste
- Warms up the brewer
- Discard rinse water before starting

## Troubleshooting Guide

### Coffee Tastes Sour/Sharp/Underdeveloped
**Cause**: Under-extraction

**Solutions** (try one at a time):
1. Grind finer
2. Increase water temperature (+2-3°C)
3. Increase brew time (grind finer or pour slower)
4. Ensure proper bloom with agitation
5. Use more coffee (1:14 ratio)

---

### Coffee Tastes Bitter/Harsh/Astringent
**Cause**: Over-extraction

**Solutions** (try one at a time):
1. Grind coarser
2. Decrease water temperature (-2-3°C)
3. Decrease brew time (grind coarser or pour faster)
4. Use less coffee (1:16 ratio)
5. Reduce agitation

---

### Coffee Tastes Flat/Boring/One-dimensional
**Possible causes**: Old beans, poor water, or uneven extraction

**Solutions**:
1. Use fresher coffee (check roast date)
2. Improve water quality
3. Increase turbulence during bloom
4. Try a different brewing method
5. Ensure even, consistent grind

---

### Coffee Tastes Weak/Watery
**Cause**: Under-extraction or wrong ratio

**Solutions**:
1. Use more coffee (1:14 or 1:15 ratio)
2. Grind finer
3. Increase water temperature
4. Pour more slowly

---

### Want More Sweetness
**Sweetness comes from balanced extraction**

**Optimal adjustments**:
1. Use **92-94°C** water temperature
2. Grind **slightly finer** (but not too fine)
3. Proper bloom: **3x coffee weight**, gentle stir, **45 seconds**
4. Target brew time: **2:45-3:15 minutes**
5. Use coffee **10-14 days** after roasting
6. Ensure **good water quality**
7. Try **1:15 ratio** for more body
8. Gentle swirl after final pour for even extraction

## Brewing Methods

### Method 1: James Hoffmann Method
**Best for**: Clarity, even extraction, forgiving technique, sweetness

**Recipe** (30g coffee, 500ml water, 1:16.7):
1. **Bloom**: 60g water (2x coffee weight), gentle swirl, wait 45 seconds
2. **Main pour**: Pour to 300g in steady circles (until 1:15)
3. **Wait** until water level drops to halfway
4. **Final pour**: Pour remaining 200g to total 500g
5. **Swirl** the V60 gently in circular motion to flatten bed
6. **Let drain** completely

**Total time**: 3:00-3:30 minutes

**Scaled for 20g**: 20g coffee, 330ml water
- Bloom: 60g, swirl, 45 sec
- Pour to 200g steadily
- Wait until half drained
- Pour to 330g
- Swirl gently
- Target: 3:00-3:30 min

---

### Method 2: Tetsu Kasuya (4:6 Method)
**Best for**: Controlling sweetness vs acidity vs strength

**Concept**:
- **First 40% of water** = Controls sweetness/acidity
- **Remaining 60%** = Controls strength

**Recipe** (20g coffee, 300ml water):

**Phase 1 (40% = 120ml) - Flavor Control:**
- **Pour 1**: 60ml, wait 45 seconds
- **Pour 2**: 60ml, wait 45 seconds

**Phase 2 (60% = 180ml) - Strength Control:**
- **Pour 3**: 60ml, wait 45 seconds
- **Pour 4**: 60ml, wait 45 seconds
- **Pour 5**: 60ml, let drain

**Total time**: ~3:30 minutes

**Adjustments**:
- **More sweetness**: Make first pour larger (70ml + 50ml)
- **More acidity**: Make second pour larger (50ml + 70ml)
- **Stronger**: Fewer pours in phase 2 (e.g., 90ml + 90ml)
- **Lighter**: More pours in phase 2 (e.g., 45ml x 4)

---

### Method 3: Scott Rao Spin Method
**Best for**: Clean cups, consistency, physics-based approach

**Recipe** (22g coffee, 360ml water, 1:16.4):
1. **Bloom**: 66g water (3x coffee), gentle stir, wait 30-45 seconds
2. **Continuous pour**: Pour remaining 294g in slow, steady stream to center
3. **Spin**: Pick up V60 and swirl horizontally to flatten bed
4. **Drain**: Let finish completely

**Key principles**:
- Gentle continuous pour (no pulsing)
- Minimal turbulence during main pour
- Spin creates flat bed = even drainage

**Total time**: 2:30-3:00 minutes

**Scaled for 20g**: 20g coffee, 330ml water
- Bloom: 60g, stir, 35 sec
- Pour remaining 270g continuously
- Spin to flatten
- Target: 2:30-3:00 min

---

### Method 4: Onyx Coffee Lab Method
**Best for**: Repeatable results, competition brewing

**Recipe** (20g coffee, 300ml water, 1:15):
1. **Bloom**: 40g water, stir gently 5 times, wait 35 seconds (ends at 0:35)
2. **Pour to 120g** by 1:00 (slow spirals from center)
3. **Pour to 200g** by 1:30
4. **Pour to 300g** by 2:00
5. **Finish draining** by 3:00-3:30

**Key points**:
- Very time-specific (helps consistency)
- Moderate agitation
- Precise pour targets

**Total time**: 3:00-3:30 minutes

---

### Method 5: Single Pour Method
**Best for**: Simplicity, clean cups, minimalist approach

**Recipe** (20g coffee, 300ml water):
1. **Bloom**: 50g water, wait 30-45 seconds
2. **One continuous pour**: All remaining 250g in slow, steady spiral
   - Pour from center outward in circles
   - Keep water level consistent
   - Don't let it drain until pour is complete
3. **Let drain** completely

**Key points**:
- Requires steady hand
- Very clean, clear cup
- Less control but simpler

**Total time**: 2:30-3:30 minutes

---

### Method 6: Three-Pour Method (Classic)
**Best for**: Balance, learning fundamentals, traditional approach

**Recipe** (20g coffee, 300ml water, 1:15):
1. **Bloom** (0:00): 60g water, wait 30-45 seconds
2. **Second pour** (0:45): Pour to 180g in center spirals
3. **Third pour** (1:45): Pour to 300g when water drops to halfway
4. **Let drain** (3:00-3:30): Complete drainage

**Key points**:
- Most common method
- Good balance of control and simplicity
- Easy to learn and repeat

**Total time**: 3:00-3:30 minutes

## Quick Start Workflow

### Day 1: Start with Baseline
- 20g coffee : 300ml water (1:15)
- Medium-fine grind
- 92°C water
- Use James Hoffmann method
- **Taste and note**: Is it sour? Bitter? Weak? Strong?

### Day 2-7: Dial in ONE Variable
- If sour → Grind finer OR increase temp
- If bitter → Grind coarser OR decrease temp
- If weak → Use more coffee (1:14) OR grind finer
- If too strong → Use less coffee (1:16) OR grind coarser

### Week 2: Experiment with Methods
- Try different pouring methods
- Keep grind, temp, ratio constant
- Find your preferred technique

### Week 3+: Fine-tune
- Adjust water quality if needed
- Experiment with temperature ranges
- Try different coffee origins
- Perfect your personal recipe

## Final Tips

2. **Take notes** on what works for different beans
3. **The grinder matters** more than the brewer - invest here first
4. **Fresh beans** make the biggest difference
5. **Practice consistency** before experimenting wildly
6. **Taste everything** - develop your palate
7. **Have fun** - coffee should be enjoyable, not stressful!
